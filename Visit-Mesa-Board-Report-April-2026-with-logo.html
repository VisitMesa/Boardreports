Executive Summary

April's story is one of aggregate lodging resilience and sales momentum. Combined hotel-plus-STVR occupancy rose to 59.3% (+5.0% YOY), driven by a surging short-term rental market that posted +10.8% YOY occupancy gains. Sales booked 9,688 rooms for the month (Leisure/OTA: 5,920 · Sports: 3,159 · National: 609), and leisure OTA campaigns contributed 30,470 FYTD contracted rooms, pushing combined FYTD rooms booked to 93,702 — +47.2% ahead of last year. On the content front, a single Salt River tubing reel reached 836K views on Instagram, one of the best-performing organic posts in Visit Mesa history.

Headwinds appeared in website traffic (sessions -7.5% YOY) and hotel ADR (-1.4% YOY). The session decline is consistent with an industry-wide shift as AI Overviews and LLM-powered search increasingly answer travel queries directly, reducing click-through to destination websites — a trend reflected in our -16.0% MOM drop in search impressions even as average position improved to 8.4. Page views held flat and pages per session increased, indicating that visitors who do arrive are engaging more deeply. The STVR market offset hotel softness with RevPAR up +13.3% YOY. Inflation climbed to 3.8% — a three-year high — with gasoline up 28.4% and airfares up 20.7%, while consumer sentiment fell to a record low of 47.6.

Market Conditions
Source: Tourism Economics · U.S. Travel Association

U.S. hotel performance improved in April as demand growth (+2.0%) outpaced supply growth (+0.4%), driving national occupancy up 1.6% and RevPAR up 4.4% YOY. However, macroeconomic headwinds intensified: inflation hit a three-year high of 3.8%, driven by gasoline prices up 28.4% YOY and airfares up 20.7% YOY. Consumer sentiment dropped to a record low of 47.6, with declines across all demographic groups. Despite these pressures, domestic leisure travel spending remained 12.9% above 2019 levels, and travel intent among higher-income households ($150K+) remained above 90%.
